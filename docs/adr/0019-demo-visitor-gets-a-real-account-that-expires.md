# A Demo Visitor Gets a Real Account That Expires

Sleevy will answer a click on `/demo` by creating a **Demo Account** — a real Account, reached without signing in, that lands the visitor in the real **Web Companion** and is deleted an hour later with everything inside it. The Web Companion already gates on one thing, the presence of an **App Session**, so a session is the whole of what a demo needs and no second interface has to exist. That is the reason for this shape rather than the obvious alternative. A canned tour on a marketing page, with fixture data and imitated interactions, costs nothing to run and nothing to defend, but it is a screenshot that moves, a reader on Product Hunt can tell, and it would put a replica of the **Inbox**, the **Library**, and the **Command Palette** beside the real ones to drift out of step with them. A wholly separate demo entity, with its own tables, was rejected for the mirrored reason: it would duplicate **Saved Items**, **Folders**, and **Sources** for something thrown away within the hour, and the claim being made here is precisely that the demo is the product.

Being an Account is inherited wholesale, so the capabilities a **Demo Account** must not have are stated rather than left to the absence of a menu item. It may not claim a **Handle**, because a Handle is claimed once and permanently, and an Account reaped an hour later would either squat one forever or release one that a **Public Profile** had already been addressed by. It may not hold an **API Key**, because that is a working credential handed to an anonymous stranger, and one that would leave a **Chrome Extension** installed and broken on their machine when the hour ran out. It may capture, but only up to a **Demo Capture Allowance**: capture behind a session nobody signed in for is otherwise a free page fetcher and a free AI proxy for whoever finds it, since every unseen **Link** costs a fetch and a model call. Its captures record the `web-companion` **Capture Channel** and register no **Source**, because a Capture Channel names the mechanism and the mechanism genuinely is the Web Companion. It is absent from the account-created analytics event, so a launch day does not read as thousands of signups.

**Demo Expiry** is one hour from creation and does not move, because a demo is a trial and an idle clock would let one visitor hold an Account all day. Three details make that hour behave. The moment is stored on the row, on the pattern the **Connect Code** already uses, and swept on a short interval rather than the hourly one beside it, so the tail after expiry is minutes instead of another full hour. The session is given the same lifetime as the row, so the cookie in the browser and the record in Postgres stop existing together — a cookie that outlived its row would present a session pointing at a deleted Account, which fails confusingly instead of by design. And every user-owned table cascades from the user row, so the sweep is a single delete: Saved Items, Folders, Sources and Profile go with it, while the shared **Links** stay, which is what keeps the **Demo Seed** paid for exactly once per environment rather than once per visitor. The Demo Seed is a checked-in list of URLs and Folder names for that reason — reviewable in a pull request, enriched through the ordinary pipeline the first time it is used, and reused by every later demo because a Link is shared across Accounts.

**Demo Conversion** carries the Saved Items the visitor captured into their new Account and leaves the Demo Seed behind, because a new Account should not open with ten items its owner never chose, while dropping the captures they did choose would be the worst first impression available at the exact moment they decided to stay. The ordering here is load-bearing and easy to get wrong: the auth library deletes the anonymous user once an account is linked, and every user-owned table cascades from that row, so the move has to finish before the delete or the delete has to be disabled. Finally, the number of live Demo Accounts per address is limited by counting rows in Postgres rather than by the **Rate Limiter** the rest of the API uses. Two things rule that out: its window is a fixed minute, so a per-day budget does not exist to be asked for, and it deliberately allows a request it cannot check against Redis. Failing open is right for reads and wrong for the one endpoint whose job is to create rows.
